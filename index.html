MAKING
THE INVISIBLE
VISIBLE.

A Ndebele woman from Zimbabwe reshaping who tells stories — and who is seen in them.

Amanda Jeannette Sibanda is a British-Zimbabwean filmmaker, writer and director from Zimbabwe. Ndebele by heritage. London by practice. She holds an MFA in Filmmaking from the London Film School — one of the world's most respected film academies — where she wrote and directed short films that screened at the London Film Africa Festival in 2012 as part of their Gems from the Diaspora programme.

Her mission has two interlocking parts: making Black, African women visible in storytelling — in front of and behind the camera — and being part of the generation of filmmakers that reshapes the image and destiny of Zimbabwean cinema. Both are urgent. Both are underway.

Most recently she has worked on two of the biggest productions in modern British cinema — The Batman (2022) and Enola Holmes 2 (2022) — gaining major-studio experience while continuing to develop her own voice as a writer and director.

"I would like to be a part of the generation of filmmakers that reshape the image and destiny of Zimbabwean cinema."
